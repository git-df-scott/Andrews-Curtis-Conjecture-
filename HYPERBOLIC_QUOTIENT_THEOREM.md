# Quotient separators and finite-certificate barriers

## 1. Exact relation and soundness theorem

Fix `F=F(x,y)`, the ordered basis `X=(x,y)`, and the exact repository tuple

```
R=AK(3)=(x^3 y^-4, x y x y^-1 x^-1 y^-1).
```

Let `N_2(G)` be ordered pairs whose normal closure is `G`. Identity entries
are allowed. On `G^2`, permit inversion, right multiplication of one entry
by the other or its inverse, and independent conjugation by arbitrary
elements of `G`. Restrict this graph to `N_2(G)` for the ACC question.
Every move is invertible and preserves normal closure: for multiplication,
recover the old entry by multiplying by the inverse of the unchanged entry;
the inversion and conjugation assertions follow directly from normality.

Left multiplication adds no power. To replace `a` by `b^e a`, first replace
it by `a b^e`, then conjugate by `b^e`. Interchange is also redundant:

```
(a,b) -> (ab,b) -> (ab,a^-1) -> (b,a^-1) -> (b,a).
```

The third arrow is left multiplication by the second entry. Thus this
convention is exactly the campaign's elementary relation. Conjugation by an
arbitrary word is a finite composition of conjugations by signed generators;
apply its letters in reverse order under the convention `g a g^-1`.

**Theorem 1 (functorial separator).** Let `pi:F ->> G` be a fixed epimorphism.
If `pi(R)` and `pi(X)` are in different AC components of `N_2(G)`, then `R`
and `X` are not AC-equivalent in `F`.

**Proof.** Evaluation commutes with inverses and products, and
`pi(w r_i w^-1)=pi(w) pi(r_i) pi(w)^-1`. It therefore takes each elementary
path to an elementary path, with arbitrary-word conjugations expanded when
necessary. Normal generation descends by surjectivity. A path upstairs would
give a path between the two images, contradicting the premise. QED.

Surjectivity is needed only to identify the precise induced conjugator group
with all of `G`. For a non-surjective map, use its image as the ambient group.
Non-equivalence even under all conjugators of a larger ambient group is still
a sufficient obstruction, but is a coarser test. Quotient equality does not
lift to equality upstairs. The theorem needs no hyperbolicity or word algorithm.

Fixing rank matters: no stabilization is allowed. Signed generator changes
are not fixed-marking image equalities. A free-group automorphism changes the
map to `pi o alpha`; a quotient survey must record this marking. A single
fixed marking with a proved separation is sufficient.

### Independent proof that the target presents the trivial group

In the group presented by `AK(n)`, let `z=x^n=y^(n+1)` and `d=xyx=yxy`.
The element `z` commutes with both `x` and `y`, hence is central. The braid
relation gives `d x=y d`, so conjugation by `d` sends `x` to `y`. Consequently
`x^n=z=d z d^-1=y^n=y^(n+1)`, whence `y=1`. The braid relation then gives
`x^2=x`, so `x=1`. This proves triviality for all positive `n`, including 3.
It gives no rank-fixed AC path. The exponent matrix for `R` is
`[[3,-4],[1,-1]]`, determinant 1; total length is 13.

## 2. Exact positive collapse in a marked infinite quotient

**Theorem 2.** Whenever marked elements `a,b` satisfy `a^2=b^3=1`, the
evaluated AK(3) tuple is AC-equivalent to `(a,b)` in their generated group.

**Proof.** First prove the identities in `G=C2*C3=<x,y|x^2,y^3>`. The table
uses only the campaign's elementary `I,M,C` operations; coordinates are 0,1,
and signed multiplication indices are 1,2 as in `acsearch.moves`.

| Step | Operation | Ordered pair, using `x^2=y^3=1` |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | evaluate AK(3) | `(xy^2, xyxy^2xy^2)` |
| 1 | `M,1,-1` | `(xy^2, xyxy^2)` |
| 2 | `M,1,-1` | `(xy^2, xy)` |
| 3 | `M,0,-2` | `(xyx, xy)` |
| 4 | `C,0,1` | `(y, xy)` |
| 5 | `M,1,-1` | `(y, x)` |
| 6 | `M,0,2` | `(yx, x)` |
| 7 | `M,1,-1` | `(yx, y^2)` |
| 8 | `M,0,2` | `(yxy^2, y^2)` |
| 9 | `C,0,-2` | `(x, y^2)` |
| 10 | `I,1,0` | `(x,y)` |

Map this path to any group generated by such `a,b`. QED.

This closes AK(3) in the standard marking of **every quotient of `C2*C3`**,
including `Delta(2,3,7)`. It also covers postcomposition of that marking by
automorphisms. It does not close arbitrary generating-pair markings of these
groups, or their full normally generating AC graphs. The path is independently
checked by free-product normal forms and integer PSL(2,Z) matrices; no finite
group enumeration was used. `C2*C3` is infinite and non-soluble (it has a
finite-index nonabelian free subgroup), and hyperbolic but has torsion.

## 3. Quantifier-free algebraic separators cannot work

The input theorem from Gilman–Myasnikov is the following **no mixed identity**
property: for a non-elementary torsion-free hyperbolic group `G`, a word with
coefficients in `G` that vanishes at every assignment of its variables is
already the identity in the free product of `G` with the variable free group.
See their [Section 3, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23031v1).
The next lemmas and corollary spell out a consequence for candidate separators.

**Lemma 3.1 (finite simultaneous avoidance).** For finitely many nonidentity
words `W_1,...,W_m` in `G*F(Z)`, there is an assignment of `Z` in `G` for
which every `W_i` is nonidentity.

**Proof.** Put `C_1=W_1` and recursively
`C_i=[C_(i-1), t_i W_i t_i^-1]`, using fresh variables `t_i` and the
commutator convention `[u,v]=u v u^-1 v^-1`. Free-product normal form shows
each `C_i` is nonidentity: the new stable letter separates two nonidentity
words in the preceding free product. If at every assignment of `Z` some
`W_i` vanished, then `C_m` would vanish at every assignment of all variables.
This contradicts the no mixed identity theorem. QED.

**Lemma 3.2 (injective conjugate substitution).** If `a,b` are nonidentity
elements of `G`, the homomorphism

```
G*F(U,V) -> G*F(p,q),  U -> p a p^-1, V -> q b q^-1,
```

fixing `G` pointwise is injective.

**Proof.** Write a reduced word in powers of `U,V` separated by constants.
Nonzero powers map to `p a^k p^-1` or `q b^k q^-1`. Their middle factors
are nonidentity since `G` is torsion-free. Different variable blocks cannot
cancel; successive blocks of the same variable separated by a nonidentity
constant contain `p^-1 g p` or `q^-1 g q`, which cannot cancel either.
Adjacent blocks without a separating constant have already been combined.
Pure constants are fixed. Every nonidentity reduced word stays nonidentity.
QED.

**Theorem 3 (quantifier-free no-go).** Let `D` be any union of nontrivial
AC orbits in `G^2`. A Boolean-valued quantifier-free formula with constants
in `G`, in the ordinary group language, which is constant along each AC orbit
in `D`, has the same value on all of `D`. In particular it cannot distinguish
two normally generating pairs.

**Proof.** A quantifier-free formula is a finite Boolean combination of atoms
`W(U,V)=1`; an equality of two terms is put in this form. An atom whose word
is formally the identity in `G*F(U,V)` is true everywhere. Collect all other
atomic words. Every nontrivial AC orbit contains a pair `(a,b)` with both
entries nonidentity: if one entry is identity, multiply it by the other.
Independent conjugation keeps `(p a p^-1,q b q^-1)` in that same orbit.
By Lemma 3.2 all the collected words remain formally nonidentity after this
substitution. By Lemma 3.1 choose `p,q` so all these atoms are false at once.
Thus every nontrivial orbit realizes the identical generic truth assignment
to the finitely many atoms. The formula has the same value there on every
orbit. AC invariance propagates that value to every point of `D`. QED.

**Scope.** This excludes finite Boolean combinations of equations and
inequations used as globally AC-invariant labels, and equation-defined closed
sets proposed to contain one orbit and exclude another. It is stronger than
testing a few raw statistics. It does **not** exclude quantified formulas,
word-language constraints, automata with more structure, or all exact algorithms.
It is not a new nonconstant invariant and does not prove AC transitivity.
The trivial tuple is excluded: the formula `U=1 and V=1` distinguishes its
singleton orbit. Domain restriction to normally generating pairs causes no
problem because every conjugate tuple used in the proof stays in that domain.

## 4. Profinite closure obstruction

**Theorem 4.** For any two-generated quotient `pi:F2 ->> G` and any two
normally generating pairs `R,S` of `F2`, the AC orbits of `pi(R),pi(S)` have
the same closure in `G^2` with its product profinite topology. Residual
finiteness of `G` is not needed.

**Proof.** Fix a finite-index normal subgroup `N` of `G`. In the finite group
`G/N`, the two image tuples are AC-equivalent: their unimodular exponent
matrices give the same abelian orbit, and the finite AC theorem applies in
rank 2. Lift the finitely many conjugators of such a path to `G`. Executing
the lifted path on `pi(R)` reaches a point congruent to `pi(S)` modulo `N`.
Intersecting finitely many such `N` handles every basic neighborhood. Hence
`pi(S)` lies in the closure of the orbit of `pi(R)`. Each AC transformation
is a homeomorphism in the profinite topology, so that closure is invariant
and contains the whole other orbit and its closure. Interchange `R,S` to
get equality. QED.

The finite input is [Borovik–Lubotzky–Myasnikov, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary
1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1103.1295v1). A continuous AC-invariant map to a
Hausdorff space therefore agrees at these endpoints: its constant fiber is
closed and contains the orbit closure. No invariant recognizable through
finite group quotients, nor a continuous profinite/pro-p refinement, separates
them. Arbitrary regular word languages are **not** automatically profinite
continuous; this theorem does not close all automata-based certificates.

## 5. What solving hyperbolic equations does provide

Fix a natural number `k`. Allow the macro moves inversion, signed left/right
multiplication, interchange, and conjugation of one coordinate by an arbitrary
element. There are finitely many move-type strings of length at most `k`.
For each, introduce variables for its conjugators and intermediate entries,
and write equations asserting every transition and the two endpoints.
Existence of that path is exactly solvability of this finite system.

[Dahmani–Guirardel, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/0901.1830v2) decides
finite systems of equations and inequations with constants in a hyperbolic
group. Thus **bounded macro-depth reachability**, with unbounded conjugator
lengths, is decidable in principle. The disjunction over all depths is not
one finite system. An unsatisfiable depth-`k` instance proves only absence
at that depth. No bound on sufficient depth for `H_SC` or `H_OR` was proved,
and no equations solver or unbounded orbit algorithm is claimed implemented.

Likewise, ordinary Nielsen algorithms preserve the ordinarily generated
subgroup, whereas independent conjugation need not. A finite-state orbit
automaton, JSJ label, relative Whitehead algorithm, or peak theorem must prove
closure under all the actual moves. A finite certificate of a closed component
may be smaller than a full orbit decider, but its closure proof cannot be
replaced by tested examples.

## 6. Peak-reduction finding and exact remaining obligation

The frozen certificate gives equal irreducible words of lengths 53/55 in
`H_SC` and 55/57 in `H_OR`. Each equality has two literal majority-relator
rewrite steps. Neither side has any majority subword. Therefore the longer
word is not geodesic, and Dehn reduction is not a canonical group normal form.
This invalidates that implementation shortcut, **not** all possible peak
theorems. The original `hyperbolic.py` docstring was already careful; the
handoff's phrase “Dehn-geodesic” was not backed by a geodesic routine.

Actual shortlex geodesics are computable using the decidable word problem:
enumerate words in shortlex order up to the input length and compare them to
the input, stopping at the first equal word. This is finite but inefficient.
Even with true shortlex forms, one still needs a theorem controlling AC-path
peaks and arbitrary independent conjugation. Small relator overlaps bound
diagram pieces, not the number of AC moves or the height of all peaks.

No assertion here decides the two remaining torsion-free target orbits.
